Treadmill pace chart: km/h to min/km

Treadmills are marked in km/h, but runners think in min/km. 12 km/h is 5:00 min/km, 10 km/h is 6:00 min/km, and 14 km/h is 4:17 min/km.

The sum is 60 divided by the speed: 60 ÷ 12 = 5.0 minutes per kilometre.

What does the incline do?

Indoors there is no headwind, and the belt pulls your feet backwards for you. Together that makes the same speed feel slightly easier on a treadmill than outside. The usual recommendation is 1% incline to make up the difference.

From about 2% upwards the incline starts changing your stride rather than just the load — the stride shortens and you land further forward. That is a fine session in its own right, but it is no longer a simulation of flat ground, and the pace cannot be compared directly to a time outdoors.

Why the treadmill feels harder anyway

Many people experience the opposite of what the chart says: 12 km/h indoors feels heavier than 5:00 min/km outside. That is rarely physiology and mostly heat and boredom. With no wind of your own making, cooling is worse and your heart rate creeps up at the same workload; with no scenery moving, your head has nothing to do. A fan and a shorter interval solve more than adjusting the speed does.
