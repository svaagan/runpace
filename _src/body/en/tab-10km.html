Why 10K is the distance runners measure themselves on

Ten kilometres is short enough to race hard without spending a week recovering, and long enough to say something real about your endurance. It also has the most round numbers to aim at — 60, 55, 50, 45 and 40 minutes are all goals worth working through in turn.

The pace typically sits 15–25 seconds per kilometre faster than your marathon pace, and 15–20 seconds slower than your 5K pace.

How to spread the effort

10K is just long enough to punish a hard opening, but short enough that you cannot sit and wait either. Spend the first two kilometres settling into the pace, hold steady from 2 to 8, and press over the last two. If you reach 8 km feeling like you have plenty left, you started too conservatively — a far cheaper mistake than the other one.
